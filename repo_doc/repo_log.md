Five points worth keeping:
  - **The force clip is an error, not a warning.** `ClampViolation` fires on any touch of the 4000 N pull-only clip, and `twin_compare` turns it into an explicitly INVALID result rather than a score, because every gradient taken through a saturated sample is a gradient of the clip. `replay.FORCE_CLIP_N`, `mjcf_generator.FORCE_RANGE_N` and `actuator_model.FORCE_CLIP_N` are pinned equal by a test.
  - **One instrument, both traces.** `ring_analysis.episodes_from_trace` is the single entry point; `twin_compare` and `fit_bounce` both go through it, so "the twin rings at the wrong frequency" cannot be two estimators disagreeing. Its `r2` is ring-beyond-trend (detrended `ss_tot`), which reads far lower than an ordinary r2 -- a clean ring inside a step settle scores about 0.3, and that is why the gate is 0.30. Measured limit recorded rather than papered over: `filtfilt` is zero-phase, so a hard onset smears the detection window backwards and pulls the fitted damping ratio from a true 0.055 down to 0.022, while the same fitter on the true window returns 0.055 to five figures. The bias is common-mode in the fit, which windows both traces identically, so an episode's zeta is a comparison and not an absolute.
  - **Everything is split by population, from the variant byte.** Per-joint and per-board metrics each carry a `tle`/`7mm` split, and `fit_bounce` fits `damp_b1` once per population (spine damping and Coulomb friction stay global -- a valve cannot change how a bearing dissipates). A joint whose two antagonists straddle the split is reported `mixed` rather than assigned. A TLE board at `0x114` is exercised in every test fixture.
  - **Both traces are referenced over the same rows or the result is invalid.** Averaging the two over what each happens to have available bakes their difference into every later number as a constant, which survives every summary statistic and reads as model error. This schema has no per-row re-zero column, so the RS485 arm's `q_zero`-steps hazard is absent here by construction rather than avoided by care.

- 2026-09-10: **`digital_twin/dataset.py` and `digital_twin/train_actuator_net.py` implemented** to `CONTRACT.md` section 7 -- 37 offline tests green (`test_dataset.py` 25, `test_train_actuator_net.py` 12), no hardware touched. `dataset.py` is pure numpy and reads a `session_*/` recording into per-cycle arrays, converting ADC with **each board's own `NodeCal` chosen from the recorded variant byte**, pushing `q` through the twin's MJCF tendons for anchored `l`/`ldot` (smoothed, then differentiated, both **per episode**), cutting multiple-shooting windows that never straddle an episode boundary, and splitting train/holdout by **whole episode** -- at 150 Hz adjacent samples are 6.67 ms apart and a per-sample split reports a holdout error that is essentially the training error. `train_actuator_net.py` is the only module in the package that imports torch: it runs the reference's shooting/BPTT objective (windows re-anchored at their own recorded `p0`, loss = mean squared pressure residual over `P_SCALE_PA`) forward in torch with autograd supplying the adjoint, so the state Jacobian is exact by construction -- on this arm `p` enters the feature row twice and a hand-written adjoint carrying only `dy/dx0` would drop a comparable, opposite-signed term and still train. Six points worth keeping:
  - **The equality test is the load-bearing one.** `ActuatorModel.net_flow_pa_s_batch` and the torch forward agree to `rtol=1e-13` over the whole feature construction -- normalisation, population bit, logistic gain blend, MLP -- including the saturated tails where a naive logistic would overflow. Nothing else makes the trained model and the stepped model the same model. A second test checks `FlowNet.forward_full`'s `dy_dp` against autograd on the same point.
  - **The regulator inside the loop is the commanded error, latched once per sync edge from the simulated ADC** (CONTRACT section 4's node-pass order), not a valve state -- that is not observable on this bus. Holding it for the cycle rather than recomputing it per substep is what produces the fill overshoot that turns a dead band into an effective hysteresis; without it the modelled valve shuts on the band edge and every closed decay the leak fit lives on disappears. The synthetic generator reproduces the same order for the same reason, and the first version of it, which regulated continuously, fitted every leak as zero.
  - **The leak fit needed a new "closed" criterion.** The reference read `ST_INFLATING`/`ST_VENTING`; this bus has no such bits, so a closed hold is inferred from the commanded error sitting inside that population's own regulation band under a constant target. Its accuracy is set by how much closed time a board got: measured on a 6-episode synthetic session, boards with >= 4 s of closed hold recover the injected leak within 7 %, boards with a single 2-3 s segment reach 37 %, and 4 of 24 boards yielded no usable hold at all and **keep their incoming value rather than being zeroed** -- the seam subtracts this number, so a spurious zero inflates an idle board.
  - **Two readers of one schema now exist and are pinned to each other.** `replay.load_recording` (scoring) and `dataset.load_session` (fitting) are asserted equal on timestamps, board type, raw counts, Pa and `q`. The single deliberate difference is the rest-offset correction: `replay` subtracts the known `0x104` +1.1 psi and `0x110` +5.1 psi because it scores against physical pressure, `dataset` keeps them because both firmwares compute their error from the board's own reported pressure. `load_session(rest_offset_psi=...)` lines them up when a caller wants that.
  - **Scale and cost, measured on the 5090.** 1 ms substeps = 7 per 150 Hz cycle; a 300-cycle window is a 2.0 s horizon, not the reference's 8 s (1200 cycles at seven substeps does not fit 32 GB, and the shortened horizon constrains the slow fill limb only through the next window's anchor -- raise it and lower `chunk` when that limb is what is being fitted). On a 5-episode synthetic session, 250 shooting epochs over 432 windows took 292 s and took held-out RMS from an unfitted 21.30 kPa to **2.10 kPa (0.304 psi)** with training RMS at 1.94 kPa. At scale -- 16 episodes of 20 s, 2880 training and 960 held-out windows, the twin's real MJCF tendons -- 120 epochs took 757 s at a **peak 2.43 GB of VRAM** (1024-window slices) and reached 3.24 kPa held out against a 22.43 kPa baseline, with training at 3.20 kPa: the train/holdout gap is the epoch budget and not memorisation, and the memory figure says the horizon could be lengthened substantially before the card is the limit.
  - Open, and stated rather than assumed: the `l`/`ldot` the net is trained on now come from `mjcf_generator`'s real tendons (excursion +-14 mm on synthetic joint motion, inside the +-25 mm bent-arm envelope) but no recording exists yet, so nothing here is fitted to this arm; `blend_width_pa` for the TLE population (6000 Pa) is still an assumption and it is what the closed-hold criterion keys off, so re-measuring it moves the leak fit; and the absolute scale of `fill_gain`/`vent_gain` is degenerate with the shared net's output scale, so no acceptance test may be written on an absolute gain -- only on between-node ratios.
